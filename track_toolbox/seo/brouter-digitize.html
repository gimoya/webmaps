Draw the route. BRouter builds the path.

This is the one. Click stations on the map and BRouter lays the track on real paths — MTB, trekking, or hiking — then that line is just another track in the list. Edit it, rename it, export GPX or KML. Same tool, phone or computer. No registration, no ads, no email, no login, free to use. Tips keep it running.

Open track toolbox

How

  1. Turn Digitize on with the route control on the map (or r on a keyboard).
  2. Pick a profile: MTB, Trekking, or Hiking. The profile applies to the next leg, so you can change it between stations.
  3. Click (or tap) each station. BRouter fills the path between the last station and this one.
  4. Save. The route is added to your tracks. Export it like any other file.

Turning Digitize off leaves the route on the map. It does not ask you to throw it away.

Pen

The pen cycles three ways. It also applies to the next leg only:

  • Route — BRouter.
  • Straight + elevation — a straight line, with elevation sampled along it.
  • Straight, skip elevation — a straight line left out of the elevation stats.

Esc while a leg is calculating cancels that leg and keeps a straight line instead. With a keyboard, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo station edits. On a phone there is no undo button for stations — Clear drops the route so you can place them again.

Where it routes

  • The clear map window is the Alps area: Austria, Switzerland, Italy and the ground around them — about longitude 5–20° and latitude 40–50°. Outside that, the map is grey.
  • A leg longer than 25 km is refused. Place another station in between.
  • Digitize is 2D only. 3D and edit stay off while it is on.
  • A new routed track has geometry and elevation from the router. It has no per-point timestamps — nothing invented a clock.

Then it is a normal track

Saved routes show up in the list with the other files. Select, rename, trim, split, merge, export. That is the point: the router is not a separate website you copy out of. It drops a track into the same toolbox.
